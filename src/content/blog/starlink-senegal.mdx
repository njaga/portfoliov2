We used to think about Internet access through cables, cell towers and fibre networks.

And now part of the conversation has moved... into the sky. 🛰️

On February 4, 2026, [Senegal's national news agency reported Starlink's rollout in the country](https://aps.sn/starlink-le-service-dacces-a-internet-par-satellite-deploye-au-senegal/).

In a place where connection quality can change from one community to another, that is bound to attract attention.

But I have a question: **does a new technology automatically mean more people can afford to get connected?**

## What satellite access can offer

Satellite broadband can reach places where deploying or maintaining terrestrial infrastructure is difficult.

Think rural communities, remote work sites or backup connectivity when other networks fall short.

That doesn't mean satellites will replace fibre and mobile networks everywhere. Different approaches can complement each other.

## Speed isn't the whole story

Download speeds make good headlines.

But users often have more practical concerns:

- What's the initial equipment cost?
- What's the full monthly bill?
- Does it need reliable power?
- Is installation and maintenance accessible?
- How well does it actually perform where it's needed?

Plans and prices change. A tariff mentioned in February 2026 shouldn't be treated as a permanent offer.

## The regulatory question

Telecommunications services don't operate outside a public regulatory framework.

In February 2026, [Senegal's regulator clarified Starlink's authorisation framework](https://www.rts.sn/actualite/detail/a-la-une/lartp-clarifie-le-cadre-dautorisation-de-starlink-au-senegal). [The national news agency later reported a five-year operating authorisation](https://aps.sn/starlink-une-autorisation-dexploitation-de-cinq-ans-accordee-au-fournisseur-dacces-a-internet-par-satellite-tutelle/).

This is part of a wider conversation about competition, regional connectivity and access to essential services.

## What I'd want to measure

I'd look beyond impressive demos and focus on a few outcomes:

- Places that actually receive a better service.
- The cost to households, schools and small businesses.
- Reliability under normal use.
- New opportunities for work, education, healthcare and information.

A service can be technically available and still out of reach for people who need it most.

## My conclusion

Satellite Internet interests me most where it addresses a real coverage problem.

But **innovation isn't only about what's technically possible. It's also about what people can now do.**

If a school works better, a small business stays connected or a community gains access to a useful service, the promise starts to become real.

The rest must be tested on the ground.
