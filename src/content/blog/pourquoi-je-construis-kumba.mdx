I've built many websites and applications over the past few years.

Enterprise platforms. Business software. Mobile applications. Internal tools. SaaS products. Personal projects too.

Some were relatively simple.

Others took me weeks or months of work.

But KUMBA is different.

Not necessarily because it is the most complex project I've built to date.

It's different because it brings together almost everything that interests me in technology today: product development, automation, artificial intelligence, data, content, and the ability to build something that can scale.

And above all, because for the first time in a long while, I don't know exactly how far this project can go.

## Initially, it was just supposed to be a media outlet

The initial idea behind KUMBA was relatively simple: create a modern platform centered around African news.

I wanted to experiment with another way of aggregating, processing, and presenting information.

Very quickly, a question arose:

**Why simply build another news website?**

The internet already has thousands of them.

The problem isn't necessarily the lack of information.

The problem is also its fragmentation.

A news story is on one media site.

A career opportunity on another platform.

A call for tenders somewhere else.

Company details on its own website or on social media.

Useful data exists everywhere, but it often remains isolated in silos.

That's where my vision for KUMBA began to evolve.

## Thinking of KUMBA as a Tech Product

Today, when I work on KUMBA, I don't really think "news website" anymore.

I think **product**.

News is a first component.

Opportunities are another.

The business directory I want to develop will be an additional one.

And behind these different interfaces, a technological layer must gradually be built that is capable of understanding, organizing, and connecting this information.

This is probably the part of the project that excites me the most.

Specifically, I'm working on mechanisms to automatically fetch information from various sources, clean it, classify it, enrich it, and then distribute it.

Artificial intelligence intervenes at several stages.

But I don't want to turn KUMBA into an "AI project" simply because the term is buzzworthy.

For me, AI remains a tool.

If a model can cut a thirty-minute task down to a few seconds, it has value.

If it can help classify hundreds of pieces of content, detect relationships between different data points, or improve search, it becomes interesting.

Otherwise, there's no reason to add it.

## The Real Challenge: Connecting Information

What I would ultimately like to build is actually quite simple to explain, even if it is much harder to develop.

Imagine a Senegalese company announces an investment.

KUMBA could naturally display the corresponding article.

But why stop there?

The platform could also know this company, its industry, its activities, and other available information about it.

It could identify key people associated with it.

Show previous news regarding the organization.

Display its job openings if it's hiring.

Reference its tenders.

Connect the information to other events in the same sector.

At that point, you're no longer just browsing between articles.

You're navigating through a network of connected information.

That idea seems far more interesting to me than a traditional media outlet.

## Why My Background Naturally Led Me to This Project

I've never really been able to choose between development, product, design, and digital strategy.

For a long time, I wondered if this dispersion was a flaw.

In hindsight, I think this combination explains many of my projects.

My experience at VIGILUS notably allowed me to tackle very diverse challenges.

I developed several digital platforms for the Group's various business lines and locations.

I worked on SEO, digital communication, content, branding, and user experience.

At the same time, continuing to write code allowed me to maintain a capability that is essential to me: **being able to turn an idea directly into a product**.

I don't have to stop at the concept stage.

I can open my editor, design an architecture, build a first version, and see a few hours or days later if the idea actually holds up.

That's probably one of the things I love most about this craft.

## KAMIT Taught Me to Think Beyond the Client Project

KAMIT represents another major milestone in this evolution.

By building solutions for various business needs, I started observing something that seems obvious in hindsight: many companies face the exact same problems.

Client management.

Sales tracking.

Employee management.

Field teams.

Real estate.

Vehicle fleets.

Invoicing.

When the same problem recurs repeatedly, building a brand new custom solution every single time isn't always the most compelling response.

You naturally start thinking in terms of product.

It is from this mindset that several solutions in the KAMIT ecosystem were born.

KUMBA ultimately applies this exact logic to a completely different domain.

I'm no longer trying to solve a single company's specific problem.

I'm trying to figure out how to build an information infrastructure that can benefit far more people.

## Building Solo Changes Everything

For now, I'm developing KUMBA alone.

That obviously means writing the code.

But in a project like this, writing code is only a fraction of the work.

You have to think about user experience.

Architecture.

Database design.

Performance.

SEO.

Automation.

AI models.

Content.

Distribution.

Security.

Infrastructure costs.

And above all, deciding what is actually worth building.

That last question is probably the hardest.

As a developer, it's easy to fall into the trap of building simply because you know how to build.

Adding a feature is sometimes far more satisfying than asking whether anyone actually needs it.

So I try to keep KUMBA's evolution relatively lean.

Build.

Ship.

Observe.

Fix.

Then build the next brick.

## AI Is Also Changing How I Develop

I also find it fascinating to build a project like KUMBA at a time when AI tools are deeply transforming our profession.

I'm not just talking about text generation.

AI is changing the way we develop software.

Research.

Prototyping.

Data analysis.

Automation.

Processing large amounts of information.

For a solo builder, this evolution is particularly impactful.

Tasks that previously required multiple specialized roles are becoming far more accessible.

That doesn't mean one person can suddenly replace an entire company.

But it significantly shrinks the distance between an idea and its first working prototype.

And for someone who probably has far too many ideas, that's quite dangerous.

## KUMBA Is Not Finished

I could present a grand, definitive vision for the project right here.

A five-year plan.

A list of game-changing features.

Millions of imaginary users.

It would probably look more impressive.

But it wouldn't be very honest.

KUMBA is still young.

Some ideas will work.

Others probably won't.

Some features I consider essential today might be removed in a few months.

The direction itself may evolve.

That's also what makes it exciting.

I'm not building KUMBA to prove I was right from day one.

I'm building it to gradually discover what it can become.

## But the Ambition Is Clear

I want KUMBA to become much more than a place where people read articles.

I want to build a platform capable of organizing a portion of the high-value information produced daily in Africa.

News.

Companies.

People.

Opportunities.

Tenders.

Events.

And progressively, the connections between all of them.

I also want to explore new ways of interacting with this data.

Traditional search will obviously be part of it.

But why not, tomorrow, be able to query this information directly?

Understand what happened in a sector over the past six months.

Identify companies hiring in a specific field.

Track updates regarding an organization.

Discover opportunities that genuinely match your profile.

Or simply understand a news item with deeper context.

That's where AI becomes genuinely interesting to me.

Not when it generates content for the sake of generating.

When it makes a vast ocean of information truly intelligible.

## I Don't Know Yet How Far KUMBA Will Go

And that's probably my favorite part.

Most projects I undertake start with a relatively clear destination.

A client has a need.

Build a solution.

Deploy to production.

Then measure whether it fulfills its goal.

With KUMBA, the destination is much more open.

I know what problem I want to tackle.

I know which direction I want to move in.

But I don't yet know the final shape of the product.

So I keep building.

One feature at a time.

One mistake at a time.

One new idea at a time.

KUMBA may only be at the very beginning of its story.

But out of all the products I've built so far, it's probably the one whose next chapter I'm most eager to discover.
