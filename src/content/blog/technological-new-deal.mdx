There's something energising about announcing a national digital strategy.

A vision. Targets. Programmes. A deadline. Usually some excellent slides.

Then the ceremony ends.

The screens go dark.

**And now, what actually changes for people?**

That's what interests me most about Senegal's *New Deal Technologique*.

## A strategy launched in 2025

On February 24, 2025, Senegal officially launched its national digital strategy as part of the broader Senegal 2050 transformation agenda.

According to [the Presidency](https://www.presidence.sn/fr/actualites/new-deal-technologique-une-ambition-nationale-pour-faire-du-senegal-un-leader-de-leconomie-numerique-en-afrique/) and [the Prime Minister's Office](https://www.primature.sn/publications/actualites/strategie-numerique-du-senegal-le-new-deal-technologique-horizon-2034), the programme focuses on digital sovereignty, public services, the digital economy and Senegal's position in Africa.

Its programme horizon runs toward **2034**, within a broader national vision for 2050.

**These are announced goals, not outcomes already achieved.**

That distinction matters.

## Four pillars, one underlying question

The strategy includes several complementary priorities:

- **Digital sovereignty**: stronger control over infrastructure, data, skills and dependencies.
- **Public services**: simpler administrative processes and systems that exchange information.
- **The digital economy**: better conditions for businesses, developers and local innovation.
- **African leadership**: stronger regional cooperation and a greater role in digital ecosystems.

These ambitions have something in common: they require far more than software.

They require organisations that can operate reliably over time.

## What I'd watch first

Imagine a citizen completing an administrative task without an unnecessary trip, without submitting the same document three times and without waiting weeks for an update.

That would be meaningful progress.

But launching another portal doesn't guarantee that experience.

Systems must communicate, procedures need simplification and staff need the tools to respond effectively.

I explore that question in [Another government portal?](/blog/senegal-plateformes-publiques-x-road).

## Small companies matter too

National strategies often focus on major infrastructure.

Yet digital ecosystems also depend on independent developers, small agencies, local service providers, teachers and founders who are still figuring things out.

As a developer trying to grow a small business, I'd love to see practical opportunities: clear rules, useful training, accessible contracts and reliable infrastructure.

Not special treatment. Conditions that help more projects exist.

## How should progress be measured?

I'd watch a few simple outcomes:

- The actual time needed to complete a public-service task.
- Connection quality across the country.
- Service availability and reliability.
- Access to digital skills.
- Real opportunities for SMEs.
- Transparent reporting on what's shipped and what is still planned.

Big goals matter, but they should not replace observations on the ground.

## My conclusion

I'd rather approach such a strategy with demanding curiosity than automatic enthusiasm or reflexive cynicism.

Launching a roadmap is one step.

**Making a service work better tomorrow than it does today is the real test.**

That's where developers, public servants, businesses and citizens all have a part to play.
