You know the moment: you're paying for an online subscription or tool, and checkout suddenly asks for a bank card number.

If you mainly use mobile money, it's a sharp reminder that **digital payments weren't always designed around your habits**.

So when a Visa or Mastercard product becomes accessible through a financial service on your phone, the conversation changes.

But a virtual card isn't magic. Let's look at what it can actually do.

## What the services offer

A virtual card can be used on merchants that accept its payment network, depending on the issuer's terms.

In Senegal, [Wave and Ecobank describe a prepaid virtual Visa card](https://www.wave.com/fr/terms_vv_sn_ecobank/) connected to an eligible Wave wallet. [Orange Money presents a virtual Mastercard](https://www.orange.sn/assistance/tutoriels/lancement-carte-virtuelle-mastercard-orange-money), introduced in November 2025. [Djamo also describes its Visa offering](https://www.djamo.com/fr-sn/carte-visa).

These products involve different partners and conditions. They shouldn't be treated as interchangeable.

## Why it matters

For a student, freelancer, developer or small business, paying online may be essential: hosting, software, training, work tools or bookings.

Making these transactions easier can create real opportunities.

But **having a card doesn't guarantee every payment will work**. Some merchants reject prepaid cards, and limits, verification and geographic restrictions vary.

## What I'd check first

- Where and for what can the card be used?
- Are there exchange, issue or transaction fees?
- How do refunds and subscriptions work?
- Can you freeze it from the app?
- What identity checks are required?

Offers change. Current official terms beat an old comparison table.

## Are banks disappearing?

Calling these “cards without banks” and declaring traditional banks finished would be misleading.

Many products still rely on banks, card networks and financial regulation.

The important shift may be simpler: **more people can access a payment method compatible with a wider range of online services**.

That's meaningful progress—as long as costs, limits and security are clear.
