Name.

First name.

Date of birth.

Occupation.

I've always found the first three fields fairly easy to fill in.

The fourth sometimes needs a little more room.

Developer. Head of digital communications. Entrepreneur. Builder of digital products.

I've learned to squeeze several activities onto one line. Sometimes I almost need to make the font smaller. 😅

But there's a question forms never ask.

**And who are you, underneath all that?**

I think that's the one I'd struggle with most.

## The person I introduce

I know how to talk about myself.

Well, the professional version of myself.

I can tell you about my career, the companies I've worked for, the applications I've built and the projects I've launched.

I know my skills. My experience. My goals.

I even have a website that brings all of that together.

Convenient.

When someone wants to know who I am, I can send them a link.

A few seconds are enough to explore several years of my life.

But sometimes that makes me smile.

I've spent so much time building my presence online that I wonder whether a search engine understands me more easily than I understand myself.

Everything is neatly organized.

Work experience on one side.

Projects on another.

Skills, certifications, achievements.

Only one section is missing.

The things I still can't explain.

## Several people in one day

At the office, I'm the person who's supposed to find solutions.

With a client, the person who has to understand a problem and suggest something concrete.

At KAMIT, the person making decisions.

With KUMBA, the person who keeps imagining what the project might become.

And then there's family.

Friends.

The people for whom I'm not a developer, an entrepreneur or the person responsible for anything.

Well, not always.

Every part of my life knows a different version of me.

Some people find me serious.

Others mostly know me for my jokes.

Some see ambition.

Others might see someone who doesn't know when to stop.

The strangest part is that they could all be right.

We like to believe we're one perfectly consistent person who simply behaves differently depending on the circumstances.

I'm not sure it's that simple.

I can be confident in a meeting and spend hours questioning a personal decision.

Give someone advice with great conviction, then fail to follow my own advice.

Want independence while secretly appreciating it when someone tells me what to do.

**I can defend an idea in the morning and question everything about it by evening.**

And I don't know whether that's a contradiction I need to fix or just part of being human.

## Always something to do

There's one thing I know about myself.

I love building things.

A website.

An application.

A company.

Sometimes even a project nobody asked me to create.

I can spend hours on a technical problem, lose track of time, and almost feel disappointed when I finally solve it.

Because once it's solved, I need to find something else.

It's a habit I know well.

In [The second workday](/blog/la-deuxieme-journee), I wrote about how my evenings sometimes turn into an extension of my workdays.

But I wonder whether there's more behind this constant need to keep moving.

Do I build because I love creating?

Of course.

Do I also build because it makes me feel like I'm going somewhere?

Probably.

And do I sometimes fill my days so I can avoid asking myself certain questions?

I'd rather not answer too quickly.

There's something comforting about a to-do list.

You know what remains to be done.

You can measure progress.

Tick a box.

Close a ticket.

Ship another version.

Life is less straightforward.

There's no dashboard showing whether you're becoming the person you wanted to be.

And even if that dashboard existed, I'm not sure I'd know which metrics to track.

## The one who does nothing

Imagine a day with no work.

No clients.

No meetings.

No project to finish.

No urgent notifications.

Nothing to produce.

On paper, that sounds wonderful.

In reality, I know myself well enough to suspect I'd eventually open my laptop.

Just to check one thing.

Then another.

And perhaps one last thing.

It's strange how difficult it can be to enjoy a moment that doesn't need to be useful.

As though every passing hour had to be justified.

I don't think work is a bad thing. And certainly not ambition.

There are projects I genuinely care about. Responsibilities I take seriously. Goals I don't want to give up.

But sometimes I wonder what would happen if, for a while, I had nothing new to say about what I'd accomplished.

If nobody asked what I was working on.

If my next project could wait.

**Would I still know what to do with myself?**

The question makes me uncomfortable.

Maybe that's why I should keep it around.

## What other people see

We don't get to choose entirely how other people remember us.

You can spend years building a reputation and be summed up in a single sentence.

*He works a lot.*

*He's ambitious.*

*He's complicated.*

*He's kind.*

*He's changed.*

That last one has always intrigued me.

Of course people change.

We learn things. Lose convictions. Meet people who influence us. Go through periods we never saw coming.

What surprises me is the unspoken expectation that we should remain faithful to a version of ourselves somebody knew years ago.

As though other people had kept an old backup of our personality.

And were surprised when they couldn't find exactly the same files.

I'm not quite the person I was at twenty.

I probably won't be the same at thirty-five.

But I couldn't tell you precisely when one version made room for the next.

There was no official update.

No version number.

Just days accumulating, and things slowly starting to matter differently.

## The field I leave blank

I'd like to end this article with an answer.

A clear definition.

A sentence strong enough to sum up what I've understood.

But that would feel a little dishonest.

I know what I like doing.

I know some of my values.

I know what makes me angry, what makes me laugh, and what can keep me working until an unreasonable hour.

I also know there are things I'm still getting wrong.

Reactions I only understand afterward.

Ambitions whose meaning changes with time.

Parts of myself I sometimes discover through other people's eyes.

None of that fits into a short bio.

Much less a form field.

So I'll keep writing, working, building and, every now and then, stopping long enough to ask myself the question.

Not necessarily to find a final answer.

Maybe just to avoid spending my whole life introducing myself without ever really meeting myself.

And if someone asks me who I am one day, without letting me talk about my job, my projects or my career...

I think I'll need a little time.

**For once, I won't rush to fill in the blank.**
